        Read with care

        What these numbers can and cannot say

        • Obligations, not cash. An obligation is the government's commitment to pay. Money is spent (outlayed) later, sometimes over many years, and a negative figure means an earlier commitment was reduced.
        • Place of performance. Every row is work performed in the USVI, whoever the recipient is and wherever they are based. A Florida firm repairing a St. Thomas pier counts; a USVI firm working in Puerto Rico does not.
        • Recent months fill in late. Agencies report contracts within days but assistance monthly or later, so the current and previous fiscal year keep changing. Older years are fixed and are tested to match USAspending to the dollar.
        • Ending is not re-bidding. A contract ending soon may be extended, replaced by an order under a larger vehicle, or not needed again. Check SAM.gov for an actual solicitation.
        • Individuals are grouped. Payments to individuals are reported in aggregate by USAspending to protect privacy, and no person is named on this page.
        • Disaster money dominates. FEMA Public Assistance for Hurricane Maria (2017) makes FY2024 and FY2025 roughly ten times larger than a typical year before the storms, so compare years with that in mind.

        How this was built

        One public source, one tested model, refreshed every day

        USAspending.gov publishes every federal contract, grant, loan and payment. Each morning the pipeline asks USAspending's bulk download service for every prime transaction and subaward performed in the Virgin Islands that changed recently, and once a week it reloads the two most recent fiscal years in full. Python lands the files into a Parquet lake, and DuckDB and dbt build every number on this page with tested SQL. One test adds up every transaction and fails the build if any completed year differs from USAspending's own total by more than a dollar. When a new contract of $100,000 or more appears, the pipeline opens a GitHub alert.
